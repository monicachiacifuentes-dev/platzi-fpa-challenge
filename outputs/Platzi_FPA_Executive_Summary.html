Platzi: fast growth, but B2C retention decides whether it's profitable

FP&A review for the CEO & CFO · Data: Jan 2023 – Apr 2024 (16 months, simulated datasets) · Prepared by Monica Chia · Interactive dashboard: platzi-challenge-3wrgxevauckcvnh6rbdvaf.streamlit.app
Bottom line
  • Growth is real and margins are scaling. MRR reached $204.7k (×5.9 in 12 months) and gross margin turned from negative to 41% as revenue outgrew the cost base. The company still runs an operating loss (−$78k in Apr-24), but it has narrowed by ≈ $9k a month since Nov-23.
  • B2B is the economic engine; B2C loses money on every paid acquisition. SMB and Enterprise earn 3–4× their acquisition cost at today's margin. B2C earns back only 0.5–0.7×.
  • Churn is the leak, and it's visible before it happens. Customers who barely use the product in a billing period are the ones who leave. A mid-cycle alert-and-save program is the fastest, cheapest lever (+$7.7k MRR in 6 months).

What the numbers say

1 · B2B carries the business: Enterprise drives MRR growth, SMB drives expansion. B2B is 69% of MRR from 21% of subscribers; in 12 months Enterprise added +$76k of MRR and SMB +$45k. At today's 41% margin, SMB returns 4.1× its fully loaded acquisition cost (payback ≈ 10 months) and Enterprise 3.0× (≈ 12 months); on the conservative 6-month margin and allowing for churn, payback stretches to ≈ 17 and ≈ 21 months. SMB also generates 4× more expansion revenue than Enterprise. Contraction is negligible (< 1.2% of starting MRR); churn is what drags NDR.
2 · Paid B2C acquisition doesn't pay back today. B2C takes 52% of the marketing budget ($790k of $1.52M). It costs $392 to win a paying B2C customer, who generates ≈ $200–280 of gross profit over their life. Marketing's "new users" are sign-ups: only ~10% become paying subscribers, so the $37 cost per sign-up looks deceptively cheap. B2C monthly subscribers churn ≈ 10% a month, and no subscriber has ever switched to the stickier annual plan.
3 · Churn is predictable, but only within the billing period. Periods with ≤ 3 active days all churned; periods with 13+ days never did (the simulated data makes this sharper than reality will be). Last month's usage does not predict this month's churn, so the signal has to be acted on mid-cycle. Of the 1,024 subscriptions renewing in May-24 ($87.8k MRR), 72 are high-risk; the named list is ready for Customer Success. Caveat: the data records usage per whole billing period, so the day 10–15 trigger must be validated with daily usage data in the pilot.
4 · Operating leverage is kicking in. Revenue grew 28× from Jan-23 to Apr-24 while delivery costs grew 1.7×, lifting gross margin from deeply negative to 41%. Each dollar of retained MRR now largely drops to gross profit, which makes retention the highest-return use of the next dollar. After marketing and G&A the business is still loss-making (−$78k in Apr-24; −$2.0M over 16 months), but the monthly loss has shrunk from −$122k in Nov-23.

12-month outlook (to Apr-25)

Base: $354k MRR (+73%), 3,119 subscribers, assuming acquisition stays flat at the last-6-month average and churn continues at recent rates (a prudent case). Bear: $320k (higher churn, flat-to-lower acquisition, half the expansion). Bull: $452k (acquisition keeps trending up and all three initiatives deliver). When back-tested on the last 6 months, the model's average error was about 10% of actual MRR (it under-forecast, because growth accelerated).

Key assumptions & caveats
  • MRR comes from subscriptions; payment amounts are list prices and don't reflect B2B price changes.
  • CAC = marketing + sales spend + a share of G&A, ÷ new paying customers (not sign-ups).
  • Delivery costs (CS, infrastructure, content) are allocated by MRR share, so all segments get the same margin %. LTV is capped at 60 months.
  • 12-month NDR rests on 417 early customers (12 Enterprise); the 6-month view is more robust.
  • Each user = one customer: B2B company domains mix segments, so accounts can't be rebuilt.
